# Scanning

**Principle:** users scan pages, they do not read them. They look for words that match their task and click the first reasonable match. Design for the scan.

| ID | Check | How to check |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | The most important thing is the most prominent | Largest, boldest, highest, or most set apart. Squint: what stands out first? |
| S2 | Related things look related | Grouped under one heading, in one box, or in one style |
| S3 | Nesting shows what belongs to what | A heading visibly covers its section. Sub-items sit inside their parent |
| S4 | The page is split into clearly defined areas | A user can decide in a glance which areas to ignore |
| S5 | Headings are plentiful and descriptive | Each heading says what the section contains. A heading sits closer to its own section than to the one above |
| S6 | Heading levels differ visibly | A level-2 heading is clearly smaller than level 1 and larger than level 3 |
| S7 | Paragraphs are short | No wall of text. Long paragraphs are split, even where the break is not strictly logical |
| S8 | Lists are bulleted | Anything that is a series of items is a list, not a sentence with commas |
| S9 | Key terms are highlighted, sparingly | Bold on the few words that matter. Not on everything |
| S10 | Visual noise is low | No shouting: competing banners, many colours, exclamation marks, animation, everything bold |
| S11 | Conventions are used for layout | Things sit where users expect them on this kind of page |
