# Home page

**Principle:** the home page has to answer the big questions before anything else: what is this, what can I do here, and why should I be here and not somewhere else. Everyone wants a piece of the home page, and those answers get buried first.

| ID | Check | How to check |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | It says what the site is | A stranger can state it after a 5-second look at the top of the page |
| H2 | There is a tagline next to the site name | One short phrase that describes the whole thing and what is good about it. Specific, not a motto: a motto could sit on any site |
| H3 | There is a short welcome blurb | One or two plain sentences on what this is and who it is for, visible without scrolling |
| H4 | It shows what the user can do here | The main things on offer are visible: what it has and what it does |
| H5 | It is clear where to start | An obvious starting point for each main goal: search, browse, or sign up |
| H6 | It gives a reason to stay | Why this and not the alternative, stated as a fact |
| H7 | Promos have not buried the answers | Banners, offers and stakeholder requests take less attention than H1 to H5 |
| H8 | The space is used for the answers | The top of the page is not spent on a stock photo or a slider with no information |
| H9 | Sign in and sign up are easy to find | Returning users and new users each see their way in |
| H10 | It hints at the depth of the site | Samples of real content, not only category names |
| H11 | A user arriving on any other page also gets the basics | Site name and tagline appear site-wide, since many visitors never see the home page |
